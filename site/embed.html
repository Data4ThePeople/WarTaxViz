How this is calculated

Costs are counterfactual, not raw inflation: what an average American household actually paid since February 27 minus what it would have paid had prewar patterns held. Gasoline uses the EIA weekly retail price against a seasonal path — the median week-by-week trajectory of 2015–2025, projected forward from the last prewar price — times average household consumption ( gallons/month, from BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey spending). Groceries and home energy use seasonally adjusted CPI (food at home; electricity + piped gas) against each category's own trailing-12-month prewar trend, applied to average household spending. Categories that ran below trend count as negative cost.

Gains are the S&P 500's move since its February 27 close ( → , ), applied to the amount you had invested, then taxed as if realized today at short-term (ordinary income) rates — 22% by default, 35% for the top 1%, 40.8% (37% + 3.8% NIIT) for the top 0.1%. Wealth-group holdings are corporate equities and mutual-fund shares per household from the Fed's Distributional Financial Accounts () — a line that excludes pension and 401(k) holdings (IRAs cannot be separated in this data) and also excludes private business equity, so it understates the wealthy's total market exposure. The median American's figure reflects the SCF finding that most families hold no stock outside retirement accounts.
